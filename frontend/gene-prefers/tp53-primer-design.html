TP53 Primer Design

PCR primer design guide for the TP53 tumor suppressor gene. Pre-configured for hotspot mutation screening, SNP-aware validation, and cancer research applications.

About TP53

The TP53 gene on chromosome 17p13.1 encodes the p53 tumor suppressor protein, often called the "guardian of the genome." TP53 is the most frequently mutated gene in human cancer, with mutations found in over 50% of all tumors. The gene spans ~20 kb with 11 exons.

NCBI Gene ID: 7157 | RefSeq: NM_000546.6 | Genomic: NC_000017.11 (7661779-7687550)

TP53 Primer Design Challenges

  • Hotspot regions: Codons 175, 245, 248, 249, 273, 282 account for ~30% of all mutations
  • High mutation density: Over 25,000 TP53 variants in ClinVar
  • GC-rich exons 4-8: Contain most hotspot codons but are GC-rich (55-65%)
  • Intron size variation: Introns 1 and 2 are very large (10+ kb)
  • Alternative splicing: Multiple isoforms affect primer design

Design Guidance for TP53 Assays

TP53 is a stress-responsive transcription factor that halts the cell cycle or triggers apoptosis; germline loss-of-function variants cause Li-Fraumeni syndrome, and somatic TP53 mutations occur in the majority of human tumours. Alterations cluster in the DNA-binding domain — the classic hotspots are R175, R248 and R273, and the IARC TP53 database catalogues published variants with their transcript nomenclature.

When the goal is variant detection, place the codon of interest inside a short amplicon with both primers in untouched sequence, and search both binding sites in dbSNP and population catalogs before ordering: a common SNP beneath one primer causes allele dropout and puzzling heterozygote failures. TP53 has several well-documented alternative transcripts, so state exactly which RefSeq your expression assay quantifies — the example pairs above target NM_000546.6 (transcript variant 1, 2,512 nt). For FFPE material aim for products of 150 bp or less; the top pair here is 162 bp, so for heavily degraded samples re-run the design with a tighter product-size window.

Practical checklist: (1) verify the binding sites in dbSNP/gnomAD so a common polymorphism does not sit under a primer; (2) confirm specificity against cDNA or gDNA as appropriate; (3) gradient the annealing temperature around 53.3 °C; (4) confirm one product of the expected size on a gel; (5) document sequence, lot, efficiency and transcript — MIQE-level reporting is what makes a result publishable.
