EGFR Primer Design

PCR primer design guide for the EGFR (epidermal growth factor receptor) gene. Pre-configured parameters, SNP considerations, and optimized primer design for EGFR mutation screening.

About EGFR

The EGFR gene (Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor, also known as ErbB1/HER1) on chromosome 7p12 encodes a transmembrane tyrosine kinase receptor involved in cell proliferation and survival. EGFR mutations, particularly in exons 18–21, are key drivers in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). EGFR spans ~190 kb with 28 coding exons.

NCBI Gene ID: 1956 | RefSeq: NM_005228.5 | Genomic: NC_000007.14 (55086724-55275031)

EGFR Primer Design Challenges

  • Hotspot mutation regions: Exons 18–21 contain common activating mutations (L858R, exon 19 deletions, T790M) requiring targeted amplicons
  • GC-rich promoter: The 5' regulatory region has>70% GC content
  • Large introns: Intron 1 alone spans ~120 kb, requiring careful exon-specific primer placement
  • Alternative splicing: Multiple EGFR isoforms necessitate transcript-aware primer design
  • Pseudogenes: EGFR pseudogene on chromosome 7 can interfere with genomic amplification

Key SNPs to Avoid in Primer Binding Sites

When designing EGFR primers, avoid these clinically significant variants:

  • rs121434568 (c.2573T>G, L858R) — Common lung cancer mutation in exon 21
  • rs121434569 (c.2369C>T, T790M) — Resistance mutation in exon 20
  • rs28929495 (c.2155G>T, G719C) — Exon 18 activating mutation
  • Exon 19 deletions (c.2235_2249del15) — Common in-frame deletions in NSCLC

Clinical Validation Required
All EGFR primers designed with VigyanLLM are for research use only. Clinical diagnostic applications require additional wet-lab validation, Sanger sequencing confirmation, and regulatory approval before patient use.

Design Guidance for EGFR Assays

EGFR is a receptor tyrosine kinase; in non-small-cell lung cancer the driving alterations are exon 19 deletions (the canonical E746_A750del among them), the exon 21 point mutation L858R, and exon 20 insertions — the last of which also harbours the T790M resistance variant. In glioblastoma the EGFRvIII variant deletes exon 2 entirely.

For these assays the mutation must sit inside the amplicon with both primers in unchanged sequence, and products should stay at or below about 150 bp: circulating tumour DNA and DNA from FFPE sections is fragmented, and long amplicons simply drop out (the example pair above is 125 bp). Allele-specific formats invert the rule — the variant base is deliberately placed at the 3′ end of the allele-specific primer so extension proceeds only from the matching allele. Check both binding sites for common SNPs before ordering, and confirm specificity against your template type: a transcript-based check cannot see genomic context.

Practical checklist: (1) verify the binding sites in dbSNP/gnomAD so a common polymorphism does not sit under a primer; (2) confirm specificity against cDNA or gDNA as appropriate; (3) gradient the annealing temperature around 53.5 °C; (4) confirm one product of the expected size on a gel; (5) document sequence, lot, efficiency and transcript — MIQE-level reporting is what makes a result publishable.
