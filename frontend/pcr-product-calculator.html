How PCR Product Size Is Calculated

The PCR product size equals the distance from the forward primer start position to the reverse primer end position, inclusive. When primer lengths are provided, the formula is: Product Size = Reverse End Position minus Forward Start Position plus 1. This gives you the exact base pair length of the amplified fragment, which determines the expected band position on an agarose gel. For example, if your forward primer starts at position 100 and your reverse primer ends at position 500, the PCR product is 401 bp.

Why PCR Product Size Matters

  • Gel electrophoresis: The product size determines which DNA ladder band your sample should align with
  • Primer validation: If the calculated size does not match your expected result, one or both primers may be binding to the wrong location
  • PCR optimization: Larger products may require longer extension times and specialized polymerases
  • qPCR efficiency: Product sizes between 70 and 200 bp are ideal for quantitative PCR applications

Frequently Asked Questions About PCR Product Size

Everything you need to know about calculating PCR amplicon size

How is PCR product size calculated?

The PCR product size is the number of base pairs between the forward primer start position and the reverse primer end position, inclusive. Formula: Product Size = Reverse End minus Forward Start plus 1.

What is an ideal PCR product size?

For standard PCR, products between 200 and 2000 bp work well with Taq polymerase. For qPCR, smaller products of 70 to 200 bp are preferred for optimal efficiency. Products over 3000 bp may require specialized high-fidelity polymerases and longer extension times.

Are primer positions 0-based or 1-based?

This calculator uses 1-based positions, which is the standard convention in molecular biology (matching NCBI GenBank, UCSC Genome Browser, and most primer design tools). If your positions are 0-based, add 1 to each value before entering them.

What if my calculated size does not match my gel result?

A mismatch could indicate non-specific primer binding (primers annealing to off-target sites), incorrect template annotation, or the presence of introns in genomic DNA templates. Verify primer specificity using BLAST and check your template sequence coordinates.
